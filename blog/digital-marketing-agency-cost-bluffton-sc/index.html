What is the average fee for a digital marketing agency?

There is no single average fee, because agencies charge four different ways: a monthly retainer, a flat project price, an hourly rate, or a percentage of ad spend. For a small local business, published 2026 benchmarks cluster around $1,000 to $10,000 per month for ongoing work, with ad spend billed separately.

Here is how the four pricing models work:

  • Monthly retainer. A fixed fee each month for ongoing work like social media, SEO or ad management. This is the most common model.
  • Project price. One flat price for a defined job, such as a website, a video shoot or an AI chatbot build.
  • Hourly rate. Usually for consulting, training or small fixes.
  • Percentage of ad spend. Some agencies charge a share of what you spend on Google or Meta ads to manage those campaigns.

Most local businesses end up with a mix: one project to fix the foundation, then a retainer to keep leads coming.

What makes the price go up or down

  1. Scope. One channel costs less than five. A one-page website costs less than a twenty-page site.
  2. Ad spend. The money that goes to Google or Meta is separate from the agency fee. A bigger ad budget does not always mean a bigger fee, but it changes the total you spend each month.
  3. Content. On-location video and photography cost more than graphics made from photos you already have.
  4. Competition. Ranking a roofing company or a real estate team takes more work than ranking a business with few local competitors.
  5. Speed. A launch needed in two weeks costs more than one planned over two months.
  6. Technology. Booking, payments, CRM connections and AI follow-up add setup time, and they often pay for themselves in saved hours.

Seven questions to ask before you sign

  1. What exactly is delivered each month, in writing?
  2. How much of my total goes to ad spend and how much to management?
  3. Who owns the website, the ad accounts and the content if we part ways?
  4. How long is the term, and how do I cancel?
  5. What will you report, and how often?
  6. Who actually does the work?
  7. Can I see results and reviews from businesses like mine?
