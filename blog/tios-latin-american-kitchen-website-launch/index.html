Tio's Latin American Kitchen website launch: one site, two restaurants, built to get the order.

Published November 25, 2026 · By Ryan Baggott · Restaurant website design for Hilton Head Island and Bluffton, South Carolina

The new website for Tio's Latin American Kitchen is live at tioshhi.com. Tio's is a family-owned restaurant with two locations, one at Shelter Cove Towne Centre on Hilton Head Island and one at Buckwalter Place in Bluffton. Our job was to give both restaurants one website that looks as good as the food and gets a hungry guest to the menu, an order or a table in a tap or two.

BaggottsBOTs Digital Marketing Agency, led by founder Ryan Baggott, designed and launched a new 26-page website for Tio's Latin American Kitchen covering both the Hilton Head Island and Bluffton restaurants. What does a restaurant website have to do?

A restaurant website has to put four things within one tap on a phone: the menu, online ordering, reservations, and directions with hours and a phone number. Everything else on the site exists to help the right guest find those four things faster.

Most people who land on a restaurant website are not browsing. They are hungry, they are on a phone, and they are deciding in the next minute or two. On Hilton Head Island that guest is often a visitor who has never been in the building. In Bluffton it is more often a regular checking tonight's special. The site has to work for both of them without making either one hunt.

What's on the new Tio's site

01Order, reserve and call from every page

Order Online, Reserve and Call sit in the header on every page of the site. Because Tio's has two kitchens, the order button opens a simple location picker first, then hands the guest to that location's online ordering. Reservations work the same way. Nobody orders dinner from the wrong restaurant.

02Seven menus, each on its own page

The full menu, cocktails and tequilas, happy hour, lunch specials, Sunday brunch, Girls Night Out and the kids menu each have a dedicated page. That is better for guests, who land on exactly what they searched for, and it is better for Google, which can match "happy hour" or "Sunday brunch" to a page that is about nothing else.

03A page for each location

The Hilton Head Island and Bluffton restaurants each get their own page with the address, hours, phone number, map, directions, ordering link and reservation link for that location. The homepage also shows nightly specials by location, so a guest sees the right deal for the restaurant they are headed to.

04Catering, events and Community Table

Catering and events have their own pages. So does Community Table, Tio's program for giving back locally, where partners can fill out the application right on the page instead of being sent off to a separate form.

05A blog that answers what people search

The site launched with posts on the questions locals and visitors actually type: the best tacos, the best margaritas and the best happy hour, each written once for Hilton Head Island and once for Bluffton. Fresh, specific, local pages are how a restaurant earns those searches.

06The story, the shop and the rest

Our Story introduces the family and the chef behind the food. The shop, rewards, gift cards and careers are all one click from the footer, so the site works for regulars and job seekers as well as first-time guests.

Built for phones first

We designed the phone version first and the desktop version second, because that is the order guests use them in. On a phone, the Call button and the menu stay at the top of the screen. Order Online, Reserve a Table and View Menu are full-width buttons a thumb can't miss. A short video of the kitchen plays behind the headline so the first thing you see is real food being made by real people.

  • Big tap targets. Every primary button is sized for a thumb, not a mouse.
  • The essentials first. Hours, location and ordering come before the long story.
  • Light, warm design. A clean cream background and the brand's orange keep the food photos as the star.

Built to be found on Google and in AI answers

A beautiful restaurant site that nobody finds is a brochure. We built the search work into the structure of the site from day one.

How it's built and managed

The Tio's site runs on SuperStack OS, our own AI Business OS. That matters for a restaurant because menus, specials and hours change all the time. A menu update or a new special is a quick edit, not a ticket that waits a week, and new blog posts and pages can be added without rebuilding anything.

Ordering, reservations, rewards and gift cards connect to the system the restaurant already uses, so the staff did not have to learn a new tool to launch a new website.

What other Lowcountry restaurants can copy

  1. Put order, reserve and call in the header. If a guest has to scroll to find them, you are losing orders.
  2. Give every menu its own page. A single PDF menu is invisible to Google and painful on a phone.
  3. Give every location its own page. Two restaurants need two sets of hours, two maps and two phone numbers.
  4. Answer the common questions in text. Reservations, parking, pickup, gluten free options. If people ask it at the host stand, put it on the site.
  5. Write for your town by name. "Best tacos in Bluffton" is a search. Be the page that answers it.
  6. Design the phone version first. That is where your guests are.
